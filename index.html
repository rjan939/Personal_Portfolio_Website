Featured Project

Two-Wheel Self-Balancing Robot

Individually designed and fabricated a balancing prototype around a REV Control Hub, a BHI260AP inertial measurement unit, motor encoders, and a custom 3D-printed chassis. The robot is modeled as an inverted pendulum — an inherently unstable system that has to be actively controlled just to stay upright.

I implemented and compared two control strategies on the same hardware: classical proportional–integral–derivative (PID) control, and a four-state linear–quadratic regulator (LQR) using pitch angle, pitch rate, wheel position, and wheel velocity as state feedback. The LQR gains came from solving the algebraic Riccati equation directly in Java. Both controllers were evaluated head-to-head using FTC Dashboard telemetry.

  • Full-state feedback via a hand-solved algebraic Riccati equation
  • PID vs. 4-state LQR compared on identical hardware
  • Custom 3D-printed chassis, IMU + encoder sensor fusion

Developed a Kalman filter for the FTC Robotics drivetrain that fuses a PID controller on measured motor velocity with a feedforward controller on applied voltage, producing a more accurate, less noise-sensitive velocity estimate for closed-loop drive control.

IR-Controlled Temperature Regulator

A fan-control system built around an NTC thermistor voltage divider, PN2222 transistor driver, flyback diode, and infrared remote. Converted 10-bit ADC readings to temperature using a Steinhart–Hart model and actuated the fan above a 71°F setpoint; verified in Tinkercad simulation and on physical hardware.

About

Why controls

I like optimizing systems — control loops, code, competition robots — but the interesting problems are usually the messy ones: the ones that don't converge on the first try, where the model doesn't quite match the hardware and you have to figure out why. That's basically what pulled me toward controls specifically, and why I'm minoring in math alongside my EE degree — state-space models and Riccati equations are more useful when you actually understand where they come from.

Outside of coursework, most of that shows up through FIRST Tech Challenge robotics, where I spent four years writing the autonomous code and mentoring newer teammates through the same control-systems concepts I was still learning myself.
